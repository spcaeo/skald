Why we built Skald, and how it compares

Skald reads your web server's own log, so it sees what a JavaScript tracker cannot: AI assistants, crawlers and every person, blocked or not. Here is why it exists, and an honest comparison with nine other tools.

Go to the comparison

The question nobody could answer

We run a small business website. More and more of the people who find a business now ask an AI assistant first. So we wanted to know three plain things. Is ChatGPT, Claude or Perplexity reading our pages? When someone asks it a question, does it come and fetch us to answer? And do the people it cites actually click through?

Google Analytics could not tell us. Not because it was set up badly, but because of how it works.

What the usual tools miss

They only count what runs their script. Google Analytics 4, Plausible, PostHog and Cloudflare Web Analytics count a visit when a piece of JavaScript runs in a browser. AI crawlers and the fetchers an assistant sends while answering do not run that script. GA4 also drops known bots on purpose: "Traffic from known bots and spiders is automatically excluded"1. Plausible filters AI crawlers out too2. How large a share AI assistants make differs from site to site. Only the server's log can count it, and a script-based tool shows none of it3.

Some people are missing as well. Cloudflare says plainly that its own analytics beacon is blocked by ad-blockers4. Plausible estimates a 5 to 25% gap without its proxy5. With GA4's basic consent mode, nothing is sent until a visitor agrees. Advanced mode sends cookieless pings and fills the gap with modelling6. A modelled visitor is an estimate, not a count.

The numbers are not live where it matters. GA4's Realtime report covers only the last 30 minutes7, and its full processing can take 24 to 48 hours8. Search Console data arrives after 2 to 3 days, with a preliminary 24-hour view910. It covers only Google, caps tables at 1,000 rows and hides rare queries9.

AI is half covered. To be fair, the tools are catching up. GA4 now has an "AI Assistant" channel, though it leaves out Google's own AI Overviews and AI Mode11. Plausible12, Matomo13 and PostHog14 have one too. But a channel only sees the person who clicks a link. It never sees the assistant reading your page to answer, or a crawler reading it to train. Bing shows citations in Copilot, but no clicks15. Search Console reports AI Overview impressions16. Tools that do see AI crawlers, like Cloudflare AI Crawl Control17 or Known Agents18, need your traffic to pass through their network or a connector.

Nobody answered the whole question in one place, from data we own.

What Skald does differently

Skald reads the one record that sees every visit: the web server's own access log.

  • Every request, sorted into five groups that add up to the log exactly: people, AI assistants, search engines, bots and scanners, and our own traffic.
  • AI split by why it came: to train, because someone just asked a question, or a person who clicked a citation.
  • Crawlers checked, not trusted. A crawler's name is compared with its owner's published addresses or DNS. A fake is filed as "claims to be GPTBot".
  • Counted twice. Every figure is recounted by a separate audit. A day that does not add up is withheld, never guessed.
  • Live. "Today so far" is redrawn every 2 minutes, the last 6 hours can be read request by request, and alerts arrive by email.
  • Private and yours. No script and no cookie in anyone's browser. It runs on your own server. Raw addresses stay in the log for 14 days, history keeps totals, and one command deletes an address on request.

What it cannot do

Keep Search Console for search queries, impressions and ranking. Keep GA4 or PostHog for clicks and events inside a page, and for ad spend. Skald also has its own limits. A page a CDN serves from its cache never reaches the log. "People" is an upper bound, because a browser's name is self-reported. AI citations are a floor, because referrers are often stripped. And you need a server whose log you can read.

So Skald does not replace those tools. It sits beside them. Even the gap between Skald's count and GA4's is useful: it shows how much the script misses.

Where Skald leads

  • It sees the machines. AI training crawlers and the fetches an assistant makes while answering someone are in the log; a JavaScript tag never runs for them, and GA4 and Plausible drop known bots on purpose.
  • It says why an AI came. To train, because someone just asked, or a person who clicked a citation; three tiers that never double count.
  • It checks names. A crawler is compared with its owner's published addresses or DNS; a fake is filed apart as "claims to be".
  • Every figure is proven. Groups add up to the log, a second count checks every figure, and a day that fails is withheld rather than shown.
  • Live, private, unblockable. Today so far every 2 minutes, each request of the last 6 hours with its reason, nothing in the visitor's browser, and it runs on your own server.

Where others lead

  • Search itself. Queries, impressions and ranking come only from Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools; Bing also counts Copilot citations, and Search Console AI Overview impressions. Skald plans an import, not a replacement.
  • What happens inside the page. Click and scroll events, funnels with in-page steps, ad spend and the Google Ads link belong to GA4, PostHog, Matomo and Plausible.
  • Reach and control. Script-based tools work on any host and count pages a CDN serves from cache; most offer named users and many sites today; Cloudflare and Known Agents can block or charge AI crawlers; Skald blocks them (since 2026-10-08) but does not charge.

Also checked

  • Fathom. JavaScript snippet, SaaS. Blocks bots including AI scrapers and does not report them; an AI tab for ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and Gemini referrals was added on 2026-10-02; no cookies; from $15 a month.86,87,88,89
  • Umami. JavaScript tracker, MIT licence, self-host or cloud. Excludes bots by default; an llm channel for ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, Gemini, Meta AI and Perplexity referrals exists in its code; cloud free to 100k events.90,91,92,93
  • AWStats. Server logs, GPL. Its robots list includes GPTBot, ClaudeBot, ChatGPT-User and PerplexityBot; batch runs from cron, not live. Version 8.0 is its author's final major release, and the author recommends moving to Matomo.94,95
  • Profound Agent Analytics. Reads server or CDN logs (AWS, Cloudflare, GCP, Vercel) rather than a JavaScript tracker; checks crawler addresses against published ranges; reports crawlers and human visits from AI. Price not published.96,97
